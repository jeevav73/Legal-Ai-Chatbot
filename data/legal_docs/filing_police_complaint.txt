TOPIC: How to file a police complaint, and what to do if police refuse to register an FIR

RELEVANT LAWS: BNSS 2023 (successor to CrPC 1973)

1. FILING AN FIR (First Information Report)
   - For a cognizable offence, the officer-in-charge of a police station is legally
     required to register an FIR based on the information given, without first
     conducting a preliminary inquiry, EXCEPT in a narrow set of situations (e.g.
     matrimonial/commercial disputes, medical negligence, corruption cases, or cases
     with abnormal delay) where a preliminary inquiry may be permitted before
     registration.
   - The complaint can be given orally (and must be recorded and read back to you) or in
     writing. You should sign it and get a free copy with the FIR number.

2. IF POLICE REFUSE TO REGISTER AN FIR
   - Step 1: Send the complaint in writing to the Superintendent of Police (SP)/DCP by
     registered post or through the police complaint portal of the state, describing the
     incident and stating that the local station refused to register it.
   - Step 2: If still no action, approach the Judicial Magistrate under the provision
     that allows a magistrate to direct police to register an FIR and investigate
     (successor to Section 156(3) CrPC, now under BNSS), by filing a private complaint
     application.
   - Step 3: A writ petition can be filed before the High Court under Article 226 of the
     Constitution seeking a direction to register the FIR, in serious/urgent cases.

3. ZERO FIR
   - If the offence occurred outside the jurisdiction of the police station you
     approached, they must still register a "Zero FIR" and then transfer it to the
     appropriate police station. Refusal to do so can be escalated the same way as above.

4. DRAFTING A GOOD COMPLAINT (structure to follow)
   - Complainant's full name, address, contact details, ID proof reference
   - Date, time, and exact location of the incident
   - Names/details of the accused (if known), or description if not known
   - A clear, chronological narration of what happened, in simple factual language
     (avoid opinions/assumptions — stick to facts and what was witnessed)
   - Names and contact details of witnesses, if any
   - List of evidence available (documents, photos, messages, medical reports, CCTV, etc.)
   - Specific relief/action requested (e.g. "kindly register an FIR and investigate")
   - Signature and date

5. ESCALATION LADDER SUMMARY
   Local Police Station -> SP/DCP (written complaint) -> Judicial Magistrate
   (Sec 156(3)-equivalent application) -> High Court (Article 226 writ petition)
   -> National/State Human Rights Commission (for rights violations)

DISCLAIMER: General information only; verify current section numbers under BNSS against
the bare act, and consult an advocate before filing any court application.
