TOPIC: Remedies when a false / malicious police case (FIR) is filed against you

RELEVANT LAWS: Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) 2023 [replaces IPC 1860],
Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) 2023 [replaces CrPC 1973],
Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA) 2023 [replaces Evidence Act 1872]

1. IMMEDIATE STEPS IF AN FIR IS FILED AGAINST YOU
   - You are entitled to a free copy of the FIR. Under BNSS provisions (successor to
     Section 154(2) CrPC), the police must give the informant/accused a copy of the FIR
     free of cost.
   - Check whether the offence is "cognizable" (police can arrest without a warrant) or
     "non-cognizable" (police need a magistrate's permission to investigate). This
     determines urgency.
   - Check whether the offence is "bailable" or "non-bailable" — this determines whether
     you can get bail directly from the police station/magistrate or need to apply to a
     Sessions Court / High Court.

2. ANTICIPATORY BAIL
   - If you believe you may be arrested in a non-bailable case, you may apply for
     anticipatory bail (successor provision to Section 438 CrPC, now under BNSS) before
     the Sessions Court or High Court. This prevents arrest while the application is
     pending/granted, subject to conditions the court sets.
   - Grounds commonly argued: no direct evidence, false implication due to personal
     enmity, willingness to cooperate with investigation, no flight risk.

3. QUASHING OF FIR
   - If the FIR appears to be entirely false, frivolous, or an abuse of process, a
     petition can be filed before the High Court under its inherent powers (successor
     provision to Section 482 CrPC, now Section 528 BNSS) to quash the FIR.
   - Courts generally quash FIRs where: the allegations, even if taken at face value, do
     not disclose any offence; there is a civil dispute dressed up as a criminal
     complaint; or there is clear evidence of malice/false implication.

4. COMPLAINT AGAINST MISUSE OF POLICE POWER
   - A written complaint can be filed with the Superintendent of Police (SP) / DCP
     against the investigating officer for harassment, non-compliance with arrest
     procedure safeguards, or failure to follow due process.
   - A complaint can also be filed with the State/National Human Rights Commission if
     there is custodial abuse or violation of fundamental rights.
   - Under BNSS (successor to Section 41A CrPC), for offences punishable with imprisonment
     of less than 7 years, police must first issue a notice of appearance instead of
     arresting directly, in most circumstances. Failure to follow this can be challenged.

5. IF THE CASE IS FOUND TO BE FALSE / MALICIOUS
   - Action can be initiated against the complainant under BNS provisions on giving false
     information / false evidence with intent to cause injury (successor to Sections
     182 and 211 IPC) if it is proven the complaint was knowingly false.
   - A civil suit for damages for malicious prosecution can also be filed, if the
     original case is dismissed/acquitted and malice + lack of reasonable cause is shown.

6. WHERE TO GET HELP
   - District Legal Services Authority (DLSA) provides free legal aid to eligible
     persons (based on income category) — every district has one.
   - The National/State Legal Services Authority (NALSA/SLSA) helpline can guide on
     free advocate assignment.

DISCLAIMER: This is general information only. Section numbers under BNS/BNSS/BSA should
be verified against the current bare act, since renumbering and amendments occur. Always
consult a practising advocate before filing any petition.
