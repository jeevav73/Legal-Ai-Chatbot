TOPIC: Free legal aid and getting official documents about your own case

RELEVANT LAWS: Legal Services Authorities Act 1987, Right to Information Act 2005

1. FREE LEGAL AID (Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987)
   - Every person is entitled to free legal aid if they belong to one of the eligible
     categories, including (not exhaustive): women, children, persons with disability,
     members of Scheduled Caste/Scheduled Tribe, victims of trafficking or disaster,
     industrial workmen, persons in custody, and persons whose annual income is below
     the limit notified by their State (this limit varies by state, typically in the
     range of a few lakh rupees per year).
   - Contact: District Legal Services Authority (DLSA) at your District Court complex,
     or the State Legal Services Authority (SLSA), or NALSA's helpline (toll-free 15100
     in most states) for free advocate assignment.

2. GETTING A COPY OF YOUR FIR / CASE DOCUMENTS
   - Most states now publish FIR copies on the state police website within 24-48 hours
     of registration (subject to exceptions for sensitive offences).
   - If not available online, an application under the Right to Information Act, 2005
     can be filed with the Public Information Officer (PIO) of the police
     department/court, requesting a certified copy of the FIR, charge-sheet, or any
     specific document. Response is due within 30 days.
   - Certified copies can also be obtained directly from the court's copying/record
     section once the case is registered there, for a nominal fee.

3. TRACKING YOUR CASE
   - The eCourts portal (services.ecourts.gov.in) lets you search any case by case
     number, party name, FIR number, or police station, and see the current stage,
     next hearing date, and orders passed.

DISCLAIMER: General information only. Eligibility limits and portal availability vary by
state; verify current details with your local DLSA or state police/eCourts website.
